**Standfirst:** Hair on the sofa is normal. Hair missing from the dog isn't.

---

If you share your home with a Labrador, a Golden or a German Shepherd, you already know the feeling: hair on the sofa, hair on your clothes, hair in corners you cleaned yesterday. "Dog hair fall" is one of the most common grooming worries in India - and most of the time, it's simply shedding.

The useful question isn't *how do I stop my dog losing hair?* It's **is this normal shedding, or is something wrong with the skin?**

## What normal shedding looks like

All dogs replace old hair; some just do it far more visibly. Double-coated breeds - Labradors, Golden Retrievers, German Shepherds, Huskies, many Indies - shed steadily and have heavier "coat blows" a couple of times a year.

Normal shedding usually means:

- hair comes out **evenly across the body**, often in tufts of soft undercoat;
- the coat underneath stays **full, with no bare patches**;
- the **skin looks healthy** - no redness, scabs, flaking or smell;
- your dog **isn't scratching, licking or chewing** more than usual.

## When hair fall is a warning sign

Hair loss that comes with changes in the skin or the dog's behaviour is different. See your vet if you notice:

- **bald or thinning patches**, especially if they're spreading;
- **redness, sores, scabs, bumps or flaky skin**;
- **itching, licking or chewing** at one area;
- a **greasy coat or a persistent smell**;
- broken, brittle hair or a dull, patchy coat;
- hair loss alongside **tiredness, weight change or other changes** in your dog.

These can have many causes - parasites, allergies, skin infections, hormonal conditions, diet - and they can look alike. A vet can examine the skin and work out which it is. More grooming or a different shampoo won't fix them.

## How to manage normal shedding at home

### Brush more, not harder

Regular brushing removes loose hair before it lands on the floor, keeps the coat ventilated and lets you spot skin changes early.

| Coat | Brush | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Short double coat (Labrador, Indie) | Rubber curry brush or deshedding tool | 2–3 times a week; daily when shedding |
| Long double coat (Golden, GSD) | Undercoat rake, then a comb | 2–3 times a week; daily when shedding |
| Long single coat (Shih Tzu) | Pin brush and metal comb | Daily |

Use deshedding tools gently and not every day - over-brushing can irritate the skin.

### Bathe thoughtfully

A good bath loosens dead undercoat so it can be brushed out. Use a mild, sulphate-free dog shampoo, rinse thoroughly and [dry right down to the skin](/journal/how-to-bathe-a-dog-at-home) - then brush while drying, when loose hair comes away most easily.

Over-bathing with harsh cleansers can dry the skin, so bathe when the coat needs it, [not on a fixed calendar](/journal/how-often-should-you-bathe-your-dog). ### Feed well, and ask your vet

Coat condition reflects overall health and diet. If your dog's coat seems dull or thin, ask your vet before adding supplements.

### Don't shave a double coat

It won't stop shedding - the hair is just shorter - and the coat helps protect the skin and regulate temperature. Brush out the undercoat instead. ([Why, in more detail.](/journal/golden-retriever-grooming-at-home))

## Shedding and the Indian climate

Many owners notice heavier shedding with seasonal change and in hot, humid months. Keep up the brushing, dry the coat fully after monsoon walks, and keep an eye on the skin - damp, matted coats are where problems start. See [the monsoon ritual](/journal/the-monsoon-ritual).

## The takeaway

Hair on the floor is usually just a coat doing its job. Hair missing from the dog - patches, sores, itching, smell - is the skin asking for a vet.

Brush regularly, bathe gently, dry thoroughly, and look at the skin, not just the sofa.

## FAQs

**Why is my dog losing so much hair?**
Most often it's normal shedding, especially in double-coated breeds like Labradors, Golden Retrievers and German Shepherds, which shed year-round with heavier seasonal sheds. If there are bald patches, sores, itching or smell, see your vet.

**How can I tell normal shedding from hair fall caused by a problem?**
Normal shedding is even across the body, with a full coat and healthy skin underneath. Bald or thinning patches, redness, scabs, flaking, itching or a persistent smell suggest a skin problem.

**Why does my Labrador shed so much?**
Labradors have a short, dense double coat and shed steadily all year, with heavier sheds a couple of times a year. Regular brushing with a rubber curry brush or deshedding tool keeps it under control.

**How do I reduce my dog's shedding?**
Brush regularly with the right tool for the coat, bathe with a mild dog shampoo when the coat needs it, dry thoroughly and brush while drying, and keep your dog on a balanced diet. You can manage shedding, but you can't stop a healthy dog from shedding.

**Should I shave my dog to stop shedding?**
No - shaving a double-coated dog doesn't stop shedding and removes the coat's protection against heat and sun. Brush out the loose undercoat instead.

**Can a shampoo stop dog hair fall?**
A gentle shampoo can help remove loose undercoat during a bath, but no shampoo stops normal shedding or treats hair loss from a skin condition. Persistent or patchy hair loss needs a vet.

**When should I take my dog to the vet for hair loss?**
When you see bald or thinning patches, red or broken skin, scabs, flaking, itching, a greasy coat or smell, or hair loss alongside other changes in your dog's health or behaviour.
