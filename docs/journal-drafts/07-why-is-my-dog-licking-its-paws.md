**Standfirst:** A little licking is grooming. A lot of licking is a message.

---

Every dog licks its paws sometimes. It's part of how they groom, especially after a walk or a meal. But when the licking becomes constant - at night, after every walk, until the fur turns reddish-brown - it's worth paying attention.

Paw licking isn't a diagnosis. It's a sign. The job is to work out what it's a sign *of*.

## Normal grooming vs too much licking

**Usually normal:** a few minutes of licking after a walk or nap, on clean, healthy-looking paws, with no limping or change in behaviour.

**Worth a closer look:**

- licking or chewing that goes on for long stretches, or wakes your dog at night;
- **rust-brown staining** on light-coloured fur between the toes (from saliva);
- **red, swollen or moist** skin between the toes or around the pads;
- a **smell** from the paws;
- **limping**, flinching when you touch a paw, or favouring one leg;
- licking that focuses on **one paw** rather than all four.

## Common reasons dogs lick their paws

### Something on or in the paw

Dirt, mud, grit, grass seeds, a splinter, a cut or a cracked pad can all set off licking - especially if it's one paw and it started suddenly after a walk.

### Moisture between the toes

Paws that stay damp after monsoon walks or baths can become irritated, and licking keeps them wet - a loop that's easy to start and hard to stop. This is one of the most common, and most preventable, triggers in Indian cities.

### Allergies

Environmental allergies (to things like pollen, grass or dust mites) and food allergies often show up as itchy paws, often alongside itchy ears, belly or armpits.

### Parasites

Fleas, mites and ticks can cause itching that dogs relieve by licking and chewing. See [ticks, fleas and the Indian dog](/journal/ticks-fleas-and-the-indian-dog).

### Skin infections

Yeast and bacterial infections between the toes often bring redness, a greasy feel and a distinctive smell. They need a vet, not a home remedy.

### Pain, boredom or stress

Dogs sometimes lick a paw because a joint or nail hurts, and some lick out of boredom or anxiety, the way people bite their nails.

## What to check at home

1. **Look between the toes and pads** in good light. Gently spread the toes.
2. **Check for anything stuck** - seeds, grit, a thorn - and any cut, crack or swelling.
3. **Check the nails** - a split or overgrown nail can hurt.
4. **Notice the pattern** - one paw or all four? After walks? At night? With other itching?

If something is deeply embedded, don't dig at it - see your vet.

## How to keep paws clean and dry

- **Clean the paws after every walk.** A no-rinse [Paw Cleaner](/products/paw-cleaner) at the door lifts surface grit and residue without water.
- **Dry between the toes** - especially in monsoon and after baths.
- **Keep the fur between the pads trimmed** so it holds less mud and moisture.
- **Avoid human creams and home remedies** on the paws - your dog will lick them off.

## When to see your vet

Cleaning and drying help with dirt and moisture - they don't treat allergies, infections or injuries. See your vet if the licking:

- lasts more than a few days or keeps coming back;
- comes with redness, swelling, sores, discharge or a smell;
- causes limping or pain;
- is focused on one paw and you can't find the cause;
- comes with itching elsewhere - ears, belly, armpits.

## The takeaway

A dog licking its paws after a walk is grooming. A dog licking its paws all evening is telling you something.

Look first, clean and dry what you can, and let your vet handle what grooming can't.

## FAQs

**Why is my dog licking its paws so much?**
Common reasons include dirt or something stuck in the paw, moisture between the toes, allergies, parasites, skin infections, pain, or boredom and stress. Persistent licking is a sign to look more closely and, if it continues, to see your vet.

**Is it normal for dogs to lick their paws?**
A little licking to groom, especially after a walk, is normal. Long or constant licking, staining, redness, swelling, smell or limping is not.

**Why is my dog licking its paws after a walk?**
Often it's simple cleaning, or a reaction to dirt, grit or moisture picked up outside. Cleaning and drying the paws after every walk helps. If it continues or one paw is the focus, check for an injury or something stuck.

**What does it mean when a dog licks only one paw?**
Licking focused on one paw often points to something local - a cut, a cracked pad, a split nail or something stuck between the toes. Check carefully and see your vet if you can't find the cause.

**What does reddish-brown fur between my dog's toes mean?**
That staining usually comes from saliva and is a sign of frequent licking. It's worth finding out why your dog is licking that much.

**Can I put coconut oil or cream on my dog's paws?**
Avoid putting human creams or home remedies on your dog's paws - dogs lick them off, and they don't treat the cause. Ask your vet what's suitable.

**When should I take my dog to the vet for paw licking?**
If the licking lasts more than a few days, keeps coming back, or comes with redness, swelling, sores, discharge, smell, limping or itching elsewhere on the body.
