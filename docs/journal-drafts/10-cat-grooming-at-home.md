**Standfirst:** Most cats keep themselves beautifully clean. Some need a little help.

---

Cats spend a remarkable part of their day grooming. Their rough tongues work like a comb, and most healthy, short-haired cats rarely - if ever - need a bath.

So, can you bathe a cat? Yes. The better question is **when does your cat actually need one** - and how to do it without stress for either of you.

## When a cat might need a bath

- **Long-haired cats**, such as Persians, whose coats can get greasy or matted.
- **Older, overweight or less mobile cats** who can't reach every part of their body to groom.
- A cat that has **got into something** - oil, something sticky, or something that shouldn't be licked off.
- When your **vet recommends** it for a specific reason.

For most other cats, regular brushing does more good than bathing.

## Brushing comes first

- **Short-haired cats:** brush once or twice a week with a soft brush or grooming glove.
- **Long-haired cats:** brush and comb **daily** to prevent mats, especially behind the ears, under the legs and on the belly.
- Brushing removes loose hair, which means **fewer hairballs**, and lets you spot skin changes, fleas or lumps early.

## Grooming a Persian or long-haired cat

Persians have a long, fine double coat and a flat face, so they need more help than most cats:

- **Comb daily** with a wide-toothed metal comb, working down to the skin. Leave tight mats to a groomer or vet - don't cut them out yourself near the skin.
- **Wipe around the eyes** gently with a soft, damp cloth; Persian eyes often water.
- **Keep the rear end clean** - a tidy "sanitary trim" from a groomer helps.
- **Bathe occasionally** if the coat becomes greasy, followed by very thorough drying.

## How to bathe a cat calmly

1. **Brush first.** Remove tangles and loose hair before any water.
2. **Prepare everything** - towels, a cat shampoo, a jug, a non-slip mat - before you bring your cat in.
3. **Use a shallow basin or sink** with a few centimetres of **lukewarm** water. Never a full tub.
4. **Wet the body gently** with a jug or cup, keeping water away from the face, eyes and ears.
5. **Use a small amount of a cat-safe shampoo** and lather lightly.
6. **Rinse thoroughly** - cats lick off whatever is left on the coat.
7. **Wipe the face** with a damp cloth instead of shampoo.
8. **Towel-dry and keep your cat warm** until the coat is completely dry.

Keep it short, stay calm and speak softly. If your cat is very distressed, stop - a waterless clean is better than a frightening bath.

## When grooming isn't the answer

A cat who **stops grooming**, grooms one area obsessively, or has a greasy, matted or patchy coat may be unwell, in pain or stressed. Hair loss, scabs, itching or a change in grooming habits are reasons to see your vet.

## FAQs

**Can you bathe a cat?**
Yes, but most healthy cats rarely need it because they groom themselves. Baths are most useful for long-haired, older or less mobile cats, or a cat that has got into something.

**How often should I bathe my cat?**
Most short-haired cats rarely need a bath. Long-haired cats such as Persians may need an occasional bath if the coat becomes greasy. Regular brushing matters more than bathing.

**Can I use dog shampoo on my cat?**
Only if the shampoo is formulated for cats too. Many dog products aren't made for cats, who lick their coats clean.

**How do I clean my cat without a bath?**
Brush regularly, use a damp cloth for small spots, and use a waterless dry shampoo made for cats for a wider refresh.

**How do I groom a Persian cat at home?**
Comb daily down to the skin with a wide-toothed metal comb, wipe around the eyes gently, keep the rear end clean with a sanitary trim, and bathe occasionally with a cat-safe shampoo, drying the coat thoroughly afterwards.

**How do I bathe a cat that hates water?**
Keep it short and calm, use only a few centimetres of lukewarm water in a basin, keep water off the face, and rinse thoroughly. If your cat is very distressed, use a waterless dry shampoo instead.

**Why has my cat stopped grooming?**
A cat that stops grooming may be unwell, in pain, overweight or stressed. If you notice a greasy, matted or patchy coat, see your vet.
